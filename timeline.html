Complete Historical Timeline

From the Kingdom of Arakan to the present day refugee crisis

8th Century

Early Muslim Settlement

Arab traders and settlers establish Muslim communities in Arakan (Rakhine), creating a diverse religious landscape alongside Buddhist populations.

Ancient History
1429-1784

Independent Arakan Kingdom

Mrauk-U dynasty rules an independent multi-ethnic kingdom with Buddhist and Muslim populations coexisting. The kingdom maintains diplomatic relations with Bengal.

Ancient History
1784

Burmese Conquest of Arakan

King Bodawpaya invades and annexes Arakan. Oppressive rule forces thousands of Arakanese (both Muslim and Buddhist) to flee to British-controlled Bengal, particularly the Cox's Bazar area.

Displacement Ancient History
1824-1948

British Colonial Period

British annex Arakan after First Anglo-Burmese War. Significant labor migration from Bengal to Arakan occurs. The region develops distinct ethnic and religious demographics.

Colonial Era
1942

WWII Communal Violence

British retreat and Japanese invasion trigger violence between Rakhine Buddhists and Rohingya Muslims. Approximately 100,000 Muslims are massacred; 22,000-80,000 flee to Bengal. 307 villages destroyed.

Violence Displacement
1948

Union of Burma Independence

Burma gains independence. Initially, Rohingya are recognized as an ethnic group under the 1948 Union Citizenship Act. Several Rohingya are elected to Parliament. However, the Burma Territorial Force conducts operations against Rohingya, forcing 30,000-50,000 to flee to East Pakistan.

Independence
1959

Mass Evictions

Immigration officials uproot Rohingya from villages in Maungdaw and Buthidaung, pushing thousands into East Pakistan. Reports of torture documented.

Displacement
1962

Military Coup & "Burmanization"

General Ne Win seizes power, ending democracy. Military junta introduces "Burmanization" doctrine prioritizing Buddhist identity. Systematic targeting of minorities begins. Refugees start fleeing to Bangladesh.

Military Rule
1974

Emergency Immigration Act

New constitution and Emergency Immigration Act provide framework for marginalization. Authorities begin confiscating National Registration Cards (NRC) from Rohingya, replacing them with "Foreign Registration Cards" identifying them as non-citizens.

Legal Exclusion
1978

Operation Nagamin (Dragon King)

Military launches census/immigration operation to identify "illegal immigrants." Widespread arrests, torture, sexual violence, and village burnings. 200,000-300,000 flee to Bangladesh. 10,000-40,000 die in refugee camps from hunger and disease. Repatriation of ~200,000 by 1979, often involuntary.

Mass Violence Displacement
1982

Citizenship Law - Statelessness Enshrined

New Citizenship Law replaces 1948 Act. Full citizenship restricted to 135 "national races" that can prove presence before 1823. Rohingya are excluded from the list, rendering them effectively stateless. Vulnerable to arbitrary arrest, forced labor, and movement restrictions.

Statelessness Legal Exclusion
1991-1992

Operation Pyi Thaya (Clean & Beautiful Nation)

SLORC military launches counter-insurgency campaign targeting civilians. Forced labor, rape, arbitrary detention, land confiscation. 250,000-270,000 flee to Bangladesh. Forced repatriation begins 1992 despite international protests. ~230,000 returned by 2005, many coerced.

Mass Violence Displacement
2012

Communal Violence & IDP Camps

Violence sparked by rape/murder of Buddhist woman. Riots and arson between communities. 192 killed (official). ~140,000 internally displaced to camps. Tens of thousands flee to Bangladesh by boat. Most remain confined in camps; return to villages denied.

Communal Violence
2014

Excluded from Census

First official census in decades conducted. Rohingya are forbidden to participate, further erasing their official existence in Myanmar.

Erasure
2015

Excluded from Elections

Democratic elections held. Rohingya excluded as both candidates and voters, despite previous participation rights.

Political Exclusion
2016

October "Clearance Operations"

Military response to attacks on border posts by Harakah al-Yaqin (ARSA). 70,000-90,000 flee to Bangladesh. Hundreds killed; mass rape and village burnings documented.

Military Operations
August 2017

Genocide - Mass Clearance Operations

Largest campaign of violence in modern history. Military response to ARSA attacks. UN describes as "textbook ethnic cleansing" and genocide. 700,000-800,000+ flee to Bangladesh. 6,700-13,750+ killed in first month (730 children). 354 villages destroyed. Failed repatriation attempts in 2018/2019.

Genocide Mass Displacement
2012-2015

Andaman Sea "Boat Crisis"

Fleeing persecution and IDP camps via smugglers. ~170,000 flee by sea to Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia. Hundreds die at sea from starvation, drowning, or abuse.

Boat Crisis
2021

Military Coup (Tatmadaw)

Junta seizes power from civilian government. Worsening security; "slow genocide" via blockades. 600,000 Rohingya remain trapped in Rakhine. ~38,000 civilians killed nationally since coup.

Historical Overview

The complete history of the Rohingya people from ancient times to present

PRE-COLONIAL ERA (Before 1824)

8th Century - Early Settlement

Arab traders and settlers establish Muslim communities in Arakan (modern Rakhine State, Myanmar). These early settlers create a diverse religious landscape alongside Buddhist populations. The region becomes a melting pot of cultures along the Bay of Bengal trade routes.

1429-1784 - Independent Arakan Kingdom

The Mrauk-U dynasty rules an independent multi-ethnic kingdom. Buddhist and Muslim populations coexist under a pluralistic system. The kingdom maintains diplomatic and trade relations with Bengal, and Muslim soldiers serve in the royal army. This period represents the height of Rohingya integration in the region.

1784 - Burmese Conquest

King Bodawpaya of Burma invades and annexes Arakan. The oppressive Burmese rule triggers mass displacement. Thousands of Arakanese—both Muslim and Buddhist—flee to British-controlled Bengal, particularly settling in what is now the Cox's Bazar area of Bangladesh. This marks the first documented large-scale displacement of the Rohingya people.

BRITISH COLONIAL PERIOD (1824-1948)

1824 - First Anglo-Burmese War

The British Empire annexes Arakan after defeating the Burmese. Under British rule, significant labor migration occurs from Bengal to Arakan, as the British encourage movement across colonial borders. The region develops distinct ethnic and religious demographics that will later be exploited for political purposes.

1942 - WWII Communal Violence

The British retreat and Japanese invasion create a power vacuum. Communal violence erupts between Rakhine Buddhists and Rohingya Muslims. Approximately 100,000 Muslims are massacred, and 22,000 to 80,000 flee to Bengal. 307 villages are destroyed. This event marks a turning point in Buddhist-Muslim relations in the region.

INDEPENDENCE & EARLY DEMOCRACY (1948-1962)

1948 - Union of Burma Independence

Burma gains independence from Britain. Initially, the Rohingya are recognized as an ethnic group under the 1948 Union Citizenship Act. Several Rohingya are elected to Parliament, and they participate in democratic processes. However, tensions remain, and the Burma Territorial Force conducts operations against Rohingya communities, forcing 30,000 to 50,000 to flee to East Pakistan (now Bangladesh).

1959 - Mass Evictions

Immigration officials systematically uproot Rohingya from villages in Maungdaw and Buthidaung townships, pushing thousands into East Pakistan. Reports of torture and abuse are documented. This marks the beginning of systematic displacement policies.

MILITARY RULE (1962-2011)

1962 - Military Coup & "Burmanization"

General Ne Win seizes power in a coup, ending Burma's brief experiment with democracy. The military junta introduces the "Burmanization" doctrine, prioritizing Buddhist Burman identity above all others. Systematic targeting of ethnic and religious minorities begins. Rohingya start fleeing to Bangladesh in increasing numbers.

1974 - Emergency Immigration Act

A new constitution and the Emergency Immigration Act provide the legal framework for marginalizing the Rohingya. Authorities begin confiscating National Registration Cards (NRC) from Rohingya, replacing them with "Foreign Registration Cards" that officially identify them as non-citizens. This is the first step toward complete statelessness.

1978 - Operation Nagamin (Dragon King)

The military launches a nationwide census and immigration operation to identify "illegal immigrants." In Rakhine State, this becomes a campaign of terror: widespread arrests, torture, sexual violence, and village burnings. 200,000 to 300,000 Rohingya flee to Bangladesh. An estimated 10,000 to 40,000 die in refugee camps from hunger and disease. About 200,000 are repatriated by 1979, often involuntarily.

1982 - Citizenship Law: Statelessness Enshrined

The new Citizenship Law replaces the inclusive 1948 Act. Full citizenship is restricted to 135 "national races" that can prove presence in Myanmar before 1823. The Rohingya are deliberately excluded from this list, rendering them effectively stateless. They are now vulnerable to arbitrary arrest, forced labor, movement restrictions, and denial of basic rights including education and healthcare.

1991-1992 - Operation Pyi Thaya (Clean & Beautiful Nation)

The SLORC military junta launches a counter-insurgency campaign targeting Rohingya civilians. Reports document forced labor, mass rape, arbitrary detention, and land confiscation. 250,000 to 270,000 flee to Bangladesh. Forced repatriation begins in 1992 despite international protests. Approximately 230,000 are returned by 2005, many through coercion and without guarantees of safety.

MODERN CRISIS (2012-Present)

2012 - Communal Violence & IDP Camps

Violence is sparked by the rape and murder of a Buddhist woman. Riots and arson spread between communities. 192 people are killed (official figures). Approximately 140,000 are internally displaced to camps. Tens of thousands flee to Bangladesh by boat. Most displaced Rohingya remain confined in camps, with return to their villages denied.

2014 - Excluded from Census

Myanmar conducts its first official census in decades. Rohingya are explicitly forbidden to participate, further erasing their official existence in the country.

2015 - Excluded from Elections

Democratic elections are held. Rohingya are excluded as both candidates and voters, despite having participated in previous elections. This represents a complete reversal of their political rights.

2016 - October "Clearance Operations"

The military responds to attacks on border posts by the Arakan Rohingya Salvation Army (ARSA). 70,000 to 90,000 flee to Bangladesh. Hundreds are killed; mass rape and village burnings are documented by human rights organizations.

August 2017 - Genocide

The largest campaign of violence in modern history. Following ARSA attacks, the military launches "clearance operations" across northern Rakhine. The UN describes it as "textbook ethnic cleansing" and later confirms genocide. 700,000 to 800,000+ flee to Bangladesh. 6,700 to 13,750+ are killed in the first month alone, including 730 children. 354 villages are destroyed. Failed repatriation attempts occur in 2018 and 2019.

2012-2015 - Andaman Sea "Boat Crisis"

Fleeing persecution and desperate conditions in IDP camps, approximately 170,000 Rohingya attempt dangerous sea journeys to Thailand, Malaysia, and Indonesia. Hundreds die at sea from starvation, drowning, or abuse by smugglers. The crisis gains international attention when boats are abandoned at sea by traffickers.

2021 - Military Coup (Tatmadaw)

The military junta seizes power from the civilian government in a coup. Security conditions worsen significantly. A "slow genocide" continues via blockades and restrictions. 600,000 Rohingya remain trapped in Rakhine State. Approximately 38,000 civilians are killed nationally since the coup.

2023 - Camp Fires & Cyclone Mocha

Arson attacks and environmental disasters destroy shelters in Cox's Bazar refugee camps. Approximately 40,000 refugees lose their shelters. A "pilot project" for return is discussed but stalls completely.

2024 - Civil War & Forced Conscription

Fighting between the Arakan Army (AA) and the military junta intensifies in Rakhine. The junta enforces conscription of Rohingya men into military service. 65,000 to 70,000+ new refugees cross into Bangladesh. In August, over 200 are killed in a massacre at the Naf River. Thousands more are killed in the broader conflict.

2025-2026 - Current Status

The crisis continues with no resolution in sight. Ongoing conflict creates famine risk in refugee camps. A surge in boat departures occurs: 3,100 attempted sea journeys between January and May 2025, with 457 dying or going missing at sea. Total refugees in Bangladesh reach 1,133,981 (April 2025). Total dead or missing since 2022: 1,878. Repatriation remains completely stalled, and the international community struggles to find a durable solution.
